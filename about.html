About

Specialist diabetes experience, with time to actually talk.

This prototype positions the practitioner as an experienced diabetes specialist nurse who is adding formal nutrition expertise, rather than trying to reinvent herself as a generic wellness coach.

Practitioner photograph

I have spent my career supporting people living with diabetes. That means I understand that the difficult part is rarely knowing that vegetables are good for you.

The difficult part is working out what to do when your blood results change, your weight is moving in the wrong direction, medication affects your appetite, family meals do not fit the plan, or you have been given several pieces of advice that seem to contradict each other.

My approach is practical, evidence-led and grounded in real clinical experience. We focus on the changes most likely to improve your health without expecting food to take over your life.

01

No judgement

Your current habits are information, not a moral failing. We start from where you are.

02

No fads

No detoxes, miracle supplements or needlessly restrictive plans.

03

Clinical boundaries

Nutrition support should complement, not replace, appropriate medical care and prescribing.

04

Useful over perfect

A plan you can follow on an ordinary Tuesday is more useful than an ideal plan you abandon in a week.

Credentials

What would go here before launch.

  • Registered Nurse / NMC status and relevant specialist experience
  • Diabetes Specialist Nurse career history
  • Nutrition qualification once selected and completed
  • Relevant continuing professional development
  • Professional indemnity arrangements
  • Any appropriate professional nutrition registration

The final version should be precise rather than padded with generic badges. The strongest credential is credible specialist experience.
